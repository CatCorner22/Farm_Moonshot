# Ethics, privacy & sensitive findings

Load in Phase 0 and before any DNA work or sensitive disclosure. When this file conflicts with speed or curiosity, this file wins.

## Scope: deceased only

- Research targets are deceased historical people. Living people's names/dates may appear only as *pointers* ("a living descendant holds the Bible") with details withheld in every output, tree, screenshot and shared file.
- If a line leads to a possibly-living person (born within ~100 years with no death record), treat them as living: stop, redact, and work the deceased generation instead.
- Do not attempt to identify, locate, contact, or profile living people — no people-search sites, social-media stalking, address/phone lookups, or contacting relatives — unless the user explicitly directs a contact step and accepts responsibility for it. Even then, draft neutral, non-pressuring outreach that reveals the minimum, and never disclose another person's DNA or parentage information in it.

## DNA privacy

- A raw DNA file (see `raw-dna-files.md`) is effectively a biometric identifier. Never upload, share, publish, commit to version control, or send it anywhere without the **test-taker's own** informed consent for that specific destination and purpose. A user cannot consent on behalf of another living tester.
- Default genetic-genealogy database settings to the most private that still serves the stated research purpose; law-enforcement matching **off/opted-out** by default; explain GEDmatch/company tier settings in plain language and record choices + dates in `05-dna.md`.
- Warn before testing/uploading: results may reveal misattributed parentage, unknown siblings, donor conception, health-relevant information, and may affect relatives who never tested. Genetic non-discrimination law is incomplete (e.g. in the US, GINA does not cover life/long-term-care insurance) — state this once, neutrally, so consent is informed, not fearful.
- Health: genealogy scope only. Do not interpret medical variants in any file (including CRAM/VCF/WGS data). If health content is encountered, note its presence, suggest qualified genetic counselling, and move on.

## Sensitive findings protocol

Applies to: misattributed parentage/NPE, adoption, donor conception, illegitimacy in eras when it carried stigma, enslavement and enslaver kinship, crime, institutionalisation, suicide, abuse, secret marriages, hidden ethnicity/religion.

1. **Verify harder, not softer:** two independent records (or DNA + documents) before presenting as more than `POSSIBLE`.
2. **Neutral, factual language:** "biological father," "father of record," "child born before the marriage," never moralising or period slurs except inside verbatim quotations, flagged as such.
3. **Private first:** deliver to the user alone, with confidence labels and what is *not* known; let the user decide onward sharing. Never surprise-contact identified relatives.
4. **Both families are real:** social/family-of-record and biological lines are documented separately and respectfully; neither is erased or called the "real" family.
5. **Distress-aware pacing:** flag that a finding may be unexpected before revealing detail ("The records point to a different biological father than expected — would you like the full detail now?") when the user hasn't already raised the possibility.

## Cultural & community care

- **Enslavement research:** name enslaved people wherever records allow; document enslavers as historical actors without euphemism; use descendant-community guidance (e.g. Beyond Kin methodology) for linking enslaved ancestors; records created by enslavers are biased sources — analyse the informant accordingly.
- **Indigenous ancestry:** tribal citizenship is a political/legal status determined solely by each sovereign nation — DNA percentages and family lore never establish it. Approach nation archives/enrolment offices respectfully, follow their access rules, do not scrape restricted records, and never present "Indigenous ancestor" claims publicly without nation-recognised documentation. Apply CARE principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) to Indigenous data.
- **Adoption & closed records:** respect sealing laws and original-birth-certificate access rules by jurisdiction; use only lawful access routes; the privacy of birth parents who were promised confidentiality limits what may be shared onward.
- **Religious/cultural sensitivities:** handle records of communities with privacy norms (e.g. church disciplinary records, Jewish records under community custodianship, Amish/Mennonite families) through their custodians' rules; some communities restrict photography, naming of the deceased, or DNA testing — ask via the user, don't assume.
- **Copyright & terms:** cite and summarise records; don't republish large extracts of copyrighted transcriptions, newspaper text, or photographs beyond fair use; respect repository and website terms of service (no scraping behind logins).

## Deliverable hygiene

Every output: living people redacted · confidence labels visible · sensitive findings flagged as sensitive · DNA upload inventory current · a note of exactly what was *not* examined so nobody mistakes partial research for proof.
