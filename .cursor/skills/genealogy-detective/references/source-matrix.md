# Source matrix — what to search, where, and what substitutes exist

Load in Phases 1–3. Rules: check every row or log `n/a + reason`; prefer images of originals; exact holdings change — verify current coverage on the repository's own site before claiming a set was searched, and record coverage gaps (missing years/parishes) in the log. Never claim access you don't have: paywalled, on-site-only, or undigitised items become precise retrieval instructions for the user.

## Jurisdiction starter notes (verify before relying; coverage changes)

- **USA:** vital records are state/county, start dates vary wildly (MA early; many states ~1880–1920); 1890 census largely destroyed (fire) — substitutes: state censuses, directories, tax. Probate & deeds at county. Naturalisation any court pre-1906.
- **Canada:** provincial vitals; 1851–1921 censuses; LAC & BAnQ (Quebec Catholic registers/Drouin), land petitions, CEF WWI files digitised.
- **England & Wales:** civil registration from Jul 1837 (GRO indexes; certificates for detail), censuses 1841–1921, parish registers (county archives), probate calendar 1858+, TNA for military/court.
- **Scotland:** Scotland's People — statutory records 1855+ (1855 certificates are exceptionally detailed), Old Parish Registers, kirk session records.
- **Ireland:** civil registration 1864 (non-Catholic marriages 1845), most 19th-c. censuses destroyed (1901/1911 survive), parish registers (NLI Catholic images free), Griffith's Valuation, tithe applotments, Registry of Deeds; PRONI for Northern Ireland.
- **Germany / Central Europe:** church books are primary pre-1870s (Archion Protestant, Matricula Catholic, state archives), civil registration ~1874–76, Standesamt privacy blocks (110/80/30 yrs), address books, emigration lists.
- **France:** état civil + parish registers in departmental archives (mostly free online), tables décennales, notarial records (minutiers) for property/marriage contracts.
- **Scandinavia:** church household-examination & moving records (ArkivDigital, Digitalarkivet, Danish Archives) — near-continuous tracking; patronymics until ~1900.
- **Eastern Europe / Jewish:** Gesher Galicia, JewishGen, JRIP-Poland, LitvakSIG, state archives; metrical books in varied languages/scripts; privacy & destruction gaps common — log them.
- **Australia / NZ:** state BDM registries, assisted-passage lists, Trove/Papers Past newspapers, convict records (Australia) with extensive kinship detail.
- **Latin America:** Catholic parish records (FamilySearch coverage strong), civil registration from mid/late-1800s by country, two-surname indexing (search both surnames).

## Access honesty

For each repository note in the log: `free | subscription (user has / does not have) | on-site only | not digitised`. If you cannot open it, give the user: exact repository, collection, identifiers to request, and what question it should answer. That hand-off is a deliverable, not a failure.
