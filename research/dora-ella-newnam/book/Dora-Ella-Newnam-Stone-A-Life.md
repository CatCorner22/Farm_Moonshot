### Preface

This book is a biography, not a pedigree abstract. It answers one question: what can be documented, under the Genealogical Proof Standard, of the life of Dora Ella Newnam, later Dora Stone, who died at Ola, Yell County, Arkansas, at noon on Monday, 23 August 1926.

Sixteen Cursor chats produced the evidence. The original mobile session opened the case and read the Arkansas death certificate the family still holds. Later sessions recovered the 1880 childhood schedule that free transcripts had missed, walked statewide Soundex reels for 1910 and 1920, tested a Peebles third-marriage hypothesis and set it aside, and photographed sixty-one frames of National Archives microfilm. This volume folds those chats into one narrative. The case dossier remains the working file; this book is the public reading of it.

Two marriages are documented. A later Peebles husband is not. The year 1868 on the death certificate is wrong. Where she spent April 1910 and January 1920 is still unknown. Those limits are part of the story.

Claim tags such as C003 and source tags such as S010 point back to the evidence catalog. They are not decoration.

## Chapter 1. The girl on Sturgeon Creek

Lee County, Kentucky, was ten years old when the census taker James J. Hampton reached dwelling 46 in District 59. The county had been cut from Breathitt, Estill, Owsley and Wolfe around the Three Forks of the Kentucky River. On 5 June 1880 Hampton listed a farmer, Elias Newnam, thirty-eight, and his wife Mary, thirty-eight, both Kentucky-born, with six children still at home: William, twenty-one, at the plough; Malissa, sixteen; Francis, fourteen; Mary, ten; Dora, eight; and Catharine, six. [C003] [S010]

That single line — Dora, daughter, eight, Kentucky — is the childhood record. A Soundex clerk later copied it onto a continuation card, code N-550, volume 18, enumeration district 59, sheet 6, line 11: “Newnam, Dora D 8 Kentucky.” [S001] [S002] A child aged eight on 1 June 1880 was born between June 1871 and June 1872. The 1900 census would give the month as August 1871. The 1885 marriage return would call her fourteen. Those three independent records agree. [C001] [S005] [S004]

Her death certificate, written by a son fifty-five years later, says 11 August 1868. That child would have been eleven in June 1880. The year 1868 is disproved. The day, 11 August, appears only on the certificate and is possible, not proved. This book uses August 1871. [C002] [S003]

The parents on the 1880 schedule are the couple the certificate would name: Elias Newnam and Mary Smallwood. [C004] Neighbors on the same page were Newnams as well — Joseph, thirty-eight, next door, and John, sixty, in the household after. Elias, known as Doll, had been bugler of Company C, 14th Kentucky Cavalry, in 1862–63. Mary Ann Smallwood, born April 1842, came from the Owsley Smallwoods. Those military and cemetery facts were gathered in the first research chat and were not re-imaged later. They remain probable, not re-proved from originals in this file.

Ten years earlier, on the 1870 Travellers Rest schedule (USGenNet transcript **7 June**; original enumerator sheet **17 June**), Elias and Mary were in Owsley County with William, Malissa, Frances and a one-year-old Mary. There was no Dora. That absence fits an 1871 birth. [C067] [S048] [S231]

Kentucky did not yet keep statewide birth registers the way it would after 1911. The surviving Lee County vital-statistics birth extracts for **1874–1878** (USGenNet Kendrick transcriptions) contain **no** line for Dora or Dora Ella Newnam. They do contain her younger sister: **5 August 1874**, **Katty Newnam**, female, born Lee County, daughter of **Elias Newman** born Clark County and **Mary Smallwood** born Owsley County. The clerk spelled the surname Newman; the family used Newnam on the census. That register names the same parents the 1880 schedule and 1926 death certificate would — and it fixes Catharine’s birth year independently of the census age. [C044] [C251] [S230] Dora’s August **1871** month and year therefore rest on the **1880 age 8**, the **1900 August 1871**, and the **1885 age 14**, not on a civil birth certificate. [C001]

A compiled Boone descent through Leah Scholl sits on WikiTree and on cemetery markers. This case has not independently re-proved those steps. They stay speculative.

## Chapter 2. Thursday at Doll Newnam’s

On Thursday, 10 December 1885, at Doll Newnam’s house, J. E. Dunagin married Samuel P. Newnam, sixteen, farmer, to Dora Ella Newnam, fourteen. Witnesses: Doll Newnam and Link Mays. It was a first marriage for both. Samuel had been born in Owsley County; his father, the clerk wrote, in Clark, his mother in Owsley. Dora had been born in Lee. The clerk put both of her parents’ births in Owsley — a slip. Elias was born in Clark County on 18 April 1842. Teenage brides and county clerks often confuse “where we farmed” with “where he was born.” [C010] [S004]

Published Lee marriage books 1–15 name **Link Mays only once**: as witness beside Doll Newnam at this wedding. He is not a groom or bride in those transcriptions. [C127] [S116] Two years later, at Marion Durbin’s on 2 January 1889, **Jiles Mays** witnessed with **Dilalrd Newnam**. That Jiles is probably the farm labourer, twenty, in Andrew Mayse’s Proctor house in 1870, the twenty-four-year-old who applied to marry Elizabeth Bowman on 22 March 1873, and the **Giles Mays** (1849, son of Andrew and Rachel, husband of Lizzie Bowman) copied at William Mays Cemetery on Elk Creek — a different man from Link. A bracketed death year of 1904 on that cemetery list is a transcriber’s note, not a proved date. [C125] [C132] [C145] [S121] [S125] [S129]

The 1900 Sturgeon transcript lists eleven **Mays** households and **no** given name **Link**. [S116] Absence in 1900 now has a paper trail, not only a nickname. In Booneville in 1880, **Louisa Pendergrass**, eighteen, was still single in Cordy Pendergrass’s house; two dwellings away, **Larkin T. Mayse**, twenty, was a single labourer in Stephen McPhearson’s. [C136] [C137] [S128] They married after that census. On 23 March 1911, at Elizabeth Brandenburg’s in Idamay, Geo. Pliman married **Ida Mays**, seventeen. The clerk wrote her parents: **Larkin Mays, Lee County**, and **Louisa Pendergrass, Owsley County**. One witness was **Sshelia Newnam**. [C142] [S132]

The 1927 Kentucky Court of Appeals said the rest in a sentence. *Brandenburg v. Petroleum Exploration*, 218 Ky. 557: **“The appellant, Louisa Brandenburg, is the widow of Larkin Mays, who died intestate on June 8, 1895,”** leaving four infant children and a Lee County tract worth less than a thousand dollars, which she still occupied as a homestead. [C139] [C141] [S131] The Cordy Pendergrass Cemetery stone says **8 July 1895**. The court says **8 June**. This book prints both and does not invent a third date. [C140]

On 1 June 1900 she was already the widow **Eliza MAYS** at Sturgeon household 26: born December 1861, four children living. Whitly, June 1884; Martha, November 1886; and Ida, October 1894, were in the house. A hired hand, John Brandenburgh, March 1882, boarded there. The fourth child, **Vesta**, had married Hardin Gray at Liza Mays’s on 4 October 1897 and was two dozen households away: Hardin, Vestie, and baby Rosco at household 85. Louisa was not in that house. [C161] [C162] [C166] [C191] [S233] Vestie Bell Gray died 21 October 1958. The Lee County death-certificate extract names her parents **Larkin Mays** and **Eliza Pendergrass**. The *Beattyville Enterprise* of 23 October 1958 names surviving husband **H. K. Grey, Arvel**. [C192] [C439] The Gray Cemetery stone on Highway 587 says 9 September 1882; the 1900 census said September 1881. [C193] [S164] Hardin K. Gray died 7 September 1967, age 91, Kentucky statewide index volume **042** certificate **20591**. The printed header on that index is **PLACE OF DEATH** then **CO OF RESIDENCE**: the row reads **FYETE** (Fayette) and **LEE**. A prior reading that put the death in Lee County swapped those columns (conflict **#27**). [C235] [C435] Identification of the index man with the Gray Cemetery stone and the 1958 paper is **probable**. [C436] The certificate image has not been examined. [C442] [C443] Martha’s 1927 death certificate names Larkin Mays and Louisa Pendergrass and gives her birth as 1 November 1886. [C164] On 29 August 1903 S. P. Farmer married Martha at **her mother’s, Eliza Brandenburg’s**. [C165] Burtie Brandenburg was born 15 July 1903, so the second marriage was in place by midsummer. [C143] [S133]

The 1910 census has two traps with the same names. In Sturgeon family 97 a **John Brandenburg**, twenty-nine, lives with a wife the Miracode clerk wrote as Louisa (the schedule itself reads **Susan**), twenty-six, and daughters Ida, six, and Bertie, five. Those ages are twenty years too young for Louisa, Ida, and Burtie. [C177] [C178] The real Martha is in the same precinct, family 251, sheet 13 B: **Samuel Farmer**, thirty-three, married six years; **Martha**, twenty-three; Flora, five; Everett, three. There is no mother-in-law in that house. [C182] Two families away on the Miracode—and on the **original** census at family **248**, sheet **13 B**—**Hardin Gray**, thirty-four, lives with **Vesta**, twenty-seven, and four children. Louisa is not in that house. Sheet **14 B** is blank; that is the only gap on the bound ED **99** roll. [C184] [C166] [C225] [S236] Kentucky’s 1910 Mays soundex (M-200) has no Lee Louisa, Eliza, Ida, or Whitley as a head. A Clark County Whitley Mats, twenty-four, boarding with Zack Nopper, is a different man. [C185] [C186] [C187] [C188] Patsey Farley’s 1937 obituary still called her sister **Mrs. Louisa Mays, Cressmont**. [C130] [S101] Cressmont is a post office, not a 1910 precinct. [C173] In April 1910 Louisa is not in Proctor under either married name. [C154] The 1911 witness **Sshelia Newnam** is not Celia McGuire, fifty-five, née Newman, in Christopher McGuire’s Proctor house that year. [C156] SortedByName’s Louisa Brandenburg block has no 1951 Lee or Owsley death; the family’s 13 April 1951 date stays family information. [C157]

Larkin was twenty-four and living on the December 1885 wedding day and dead before June 1900. **Link** is a common nickname for Larkin. That is enough for a possible identity, not a merge: no paper yet uses both names. [C131] They married after the 1880 Booneville census and before Vestie, about September 1881 or 1882 — not merely before Ida. [C483] Published Owsley marriage vital-statistics years — 1853–59, 1874–78, 1903–10 — do not contain their license. Lee Books 1–37 have no party row. The published gaps are 1879–1902 and also 1905–1907. [C148] [C475] [C476] [S126] [S412] The Owsley courthouse burned on 29 January 1929; KDLA also lists a 1967 disaster. About five thousand marriage records were lost. [C471] [C472] [S334] The archived RootsWeb groom and bride tables that do exist cover 1852–1879 and have no Larkin × Louisa or Robert × Sallie row. Louisa Flanary married William Mays on 22 January 1874 — a different couple. The 1880–1920 compiled pages were never published on that index. [C518] [C519] [C520] [C521] Hayes’s reconstructed A–L book (ISBN 9780788410581, FamilySearch 818120) and Smith’s 1843–1929 compilation (FamilySearch 733757) are catalogued and unread. Hayes dates are estimates from the oldest child plus one year. This book does not invent rows from books that were not opened. [C473] [C474] [C482] [C522]

Robert and Sallie Newnam, parents of infant Lester at Pebworth on 15 April 1909, are still not merged with the Farley-stepson Robert of 1900 or with the 1910 Buck Creek “UNUM Robert” who had a wife Martha. [C108] [C116] [C119] They are not a 1910 Owsley head-and-wife household on any published district transcript, and they are not Owsley death-certificate extract decedents in 1911–1945. [C477] [C479] A license in the 1905–1907 published gap remains possible. [C475]

Mays were already in District 59 in 1880. The mortality schedule Hampton filed for that district lists **Mary Mays**, eighty-three, of household **102**, dead of paralysis in January. [C133] [S120] William Mays Cemetery, just over the Lee line at Cross Roads, has **Mary A. “Pollie” Mays**, 1800–1880, née Gross. Ages 80 and 83 are close; the birthplaces do not yet match. She is a possible neighbour, not a merge. [C147] Sheets 1–2 around John and Elias do not show household 102; the original page is still blocked on Internet Archive. [L136]

A separate **Newnam Cemetery** at Pebworth — also called Newman or Newman-Combs, a Kentucky Landmark, 101 Find a Grave memorials — is the earlier clan yard, not High View on Spencer Ridge. The 22 February 2006 reading by J. E. Combs, James Cornett, Thomas Cornett, and Mark Edwards at Crossroads Tom Cornett Farm names the earlier clan (Leah Scholl, the Johns, Richard Morris, Lucinda Steele 1839–1906, Frank, infants of Frank) and **does not name Samuel P.** Find A Grave’s 20 Newnam listings agree. The USGenWeb Archives cemetery directory never carried that 2006 file. A marked Samuel stone there is **probable-absent**. An unmarked grave, a stone the 2006 party missed, or burial somewhere else remains **possible**. This book does not invent a stone. [C150] [C525] [C526] [C533] [S130] [S457] [S459]

The original marriage page (FamilySearch Q2QD-PZHL) has not been seen from this workspace. The reading is from Debi Kendrick’s Lee County Book 2 transcription. The fact of the marriage is proved; palaeography of the original page remains an open retrieval.

Samuel was family. On the first page of the same 1880 district, dwelling 3, taken 1 June, a boy named Samuel, eleven, son, lived with John Newnam, sixty-nine, farmer, born in Kentucky, father born in North Carolina, and Mary, fifty-seven, with Eli, sixteen, Susan, twenty, and Hannah, nine. [C060] [C061] [S040] Age eleven in June 1880 is exactly the June 1869 birth of the 1900 husband and the sixteen-year-old of December 1885. Identification of that boy as Dora’s husband is probable.

Ten years before that, on 29 June 1870, Sturgeon Precinct household 19 held Newman, John, fifty, farm labourer; Elizabeth, twenty-five, keeping house; and Samuel, one, with eight others. [C062] [S042] That Elizabeth is the right age for the widow who later took Pearl. The 1870 and 1880 households are not the same house with a bad enumerator. John ages fifty to sixty-nine in ten years. The wives are Elizabeth, twenty-five, and Mary, fifty-seven. The children’s names do not carry over. Conflict #9 stays open. This book does not print “son of Elizabeth” or “son of Mary” as fact.

A second Samuel, about thirteen, on sheet 2 of the same district is Samuel McQueary, not a Newnam. [C063] There is only one Samuel Newnam of the right age in ED 59.

WikiTree gives Samuel the middle name Parriott. No record in this case uses it. The initial P. is proved; the expansion is lore.

## Chapter 3. Seven born, five living

They stayed on Sturgeon Creek. On 1 June 1900 Samuel, born June 1869, married fourteen years, and Dora E., born August 1871, twenty-eight, stood at dwelling 67 with five children: McCoy, April 1890; Mary M., June 1892; Pearl, March 1895; Rissa, May 1897; and John H., August 1899, nine months old. Dora reported seven children born, five living. [C011] [C020–C025] [S005] The two who died are unnamed in any record yet read. Two deaths in seven births is a household fact, not a county rate.

The original 1900 population schedule is not among the sixty-one plates. The household is known from a reliable county transcription (S005). Recovering the microfilm page remains outstanding.

Pearl later opened a Social Security account in her married name, Pearl P. Jackson, also known as Pearl P. Newnam. She named her parents Sam Newnam and Dora Newnam, and her birthplace Banford, Lee County, 15 March 1895. Banford was the post office on Sturgeon Creek from 1902 to 1934. She died in Shelby County, Tennessee, on 19 September 1980. [S007] Mary Magdalene’s account names Sam P. and Dora Newnam and Beattyville, 12 June 1893 — a year off the 1900 census, a common childhood discrepancy. She is later Mary M. Bunch. [S006]

Those NUMIDENT files were read in the first research chat. They close identity for two daughters. They do not locate the mother after 1900.

## Chapter 4. The years the census lost

Samuel disappears from free records. WikiTree’s 24 February 1906 is unsourced lore and is not printed here as a death date. [C012] [S030] What is not lore is the next sighting of a child.

In April 1910 Pearl, fifteen, was in Owsley County, Buck Creek, household 141, with her widowed paternal grandmother Elizabeth Newman, sixty-one, who reported seven children born and two living, and with Susan Angle, forty-four, a widow. [S054] Pearl married Clyde Jackson; their daughters Margaret E. (29 June 1917) and Fannie B. (11 March 1919) were registered in Lee County with mother Pearl Newman. By 1920 Clyde, twenty-six, and Pearl, twenty-four, were back on Sturgeon Creek with Estella and Fannie.

## How to read the plates

Part II of the PDF prints every JPEG preserved in this case, sixty-one frames, in chronological order of the record. Four roles appear in the captions:

- **Family hit** — names Dora or a documented relative.
- **Rejected same-name** — a Dora, Samuel, Thomas, or similar who fails the same-person test.
- **Negative search** — the alphabetical or dwelling slot where she would appear. She is not there. Negatives are evidence.
- **Context** — an adjacent page or divider, kept so a later reader can verify the search window.

The plates are National Archives microfilm via the Internet Archive, inverted and contrast-enhanced where the scan required it. They are not studio photographs.
