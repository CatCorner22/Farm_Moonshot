Elmer GABBARD

Beattyville Enterprise
Thursday, 23 June 1960

Dr. Elmer C. Gabbard Claimed Following Very Brief Illness

Death claimed Dr. Elmer C. Gabbard Friday night, June 17th in a Hazard Hospital following a short illness.

The former president of Buckhorn School, one of nine children, was born at Ricetown in Owsley County, October 9, 1880.  He received his elementary education at Buckhorn, spent one year at Centre College, transferred to Berea where he graduated in 1913.  Following a three year course at the Presbyterian Theological Seminary of Louisville, he returned to his "Calling", the Buckhorn School.  In 1928 he was awarded a Doctor of Divinity degree from Kings College, Bristol, TN.

Dr. Gabbard refused many positions of high honor to remain at Buckhorn, among which was a Deanship at Berea College.  He did leave Buckhorn to pastor a church in Knoxville and later in Chattanooga, but in 1936, upon the death of Dr. Murdoch, he eagerly accepted the call to return to Buckhorn, where he remained until his retirement in 1956.

Since 1920, he has been a member of the Board of Trustees of Berea College, in addition he served on the Board of Maryville College, the Presbyterian Theological Seminary and Buckhorn.  He was a mason.

He is survived by his wife, Mrs. Myrtle Ward Gabbard, two daughters, Mrs. Rice J. Kirby and Mrs. Clarence Maggard, both of Hazard and three grandchildren.

Final rites were conducted at the Hull Memorial Church at 1 p.m. Sunday, June 19th by Dr. Arthur GROSS and Rev. Ernest STRICKLIN.  Burial followed at the Berea cemetery.

Dr. Fred Adkin GLASS

The Beattyville Enterprise
Thursday, 21 November 1957

Native Owsley Native Passes in Oklahoma

Dr. Fred Adkin Glass, 70, long-time Tulsa physician and surgeon, died recently at his home northeast of Tulsa following a short illness.

Dr. Glass was born March 11, 1887, in Travellers Rest, and moved to Tulsa in 1915.  He was named the first chief of staff of St. John's hospital when it opened in 1926.

He graduated from Centre College, Danville, Ky., where he played on the football team.  A graduate of John Hopkins School of Medicine in Baltimore in 1912, Dr. Glass took his residency in surgery at Lakeside hospital in Cleveland, O., under Dr. George Crile.

In 1916, following his move to Tulsa, he married Ruth Huntington Soutar in Sandusky, O.  The widow survives Dr. Glass.

In 1947, Dr. Glass, in association with Dr. Frank J. Nelson, founded the Glass-Nelson Clinic in Tulsa.

The career of Dr. Glass as a surgeon and doctor was highly colorful since he started practicing medicine during the early days in this area.  The first X-ray machine in Tulsa was purchased and operated by him and he was credited with many of the "firsts" in surgery performed in the early 20s.

Dr. Glass, who suffered a crippling X-ray burn to his right hand as a youth, became one the southwest's outstanding surgeons.  He was a member of the Tulsa County Medical Society, the Oklahoma State Medical Assn., American Medical Assn., and the America College of Surgeons.

He was a member of the Methodist church and was a former member of The Tulsa Club and Southern Hills Country Club.

Survivors, in addition to the widow, are his mother, Mrs. Lucy Glass, Sand Springs; two sisters, Mrs. Fanny Johnson, of Sand Springs, and Mrs. Paul Edwards, Tulsa; a daughter, Mary Glass, and two sons, Robert Adkin Glass and Fred Adkin Glass, Jr., all of Tulsa, and two grandchildren, Leslie and Gary Glass.

Lexington Herald-Leader (Lexington, KY)
Saturday, 16 December 1933

Judge George W. Gourley, 81, Beattyville and Lexington, pioneer in the good-roads movement in eastern Kentucky and widely known jurist, died at 8:15 o'clock this morning at St. Joseph's hospital after a week's illness.

Judge Gourley was stricken with a blood infection in Beattyville a week ago and was removed to the hospital here for treatment.  His Lexington residence was at 216 east High street.

Judge Gourley served as chairman of the eastern and central division of the Kentucky Good Roads Association in 1924, and was a member of the National Good Roads Association committee which called on President Coolidge.  He worked for years in the interest of good roads in central and eastern Kentucky and lived to see the fruits of his labor in the contruction of miles of hard-surfaced highways.

Judge Gourley served as county judge of Lee county from 1874 to 1878, and for 30 years was attorney for the Louisville and Nashville railway company in its eastern Kentucky division.  He also was chairman of the Lee county Republican organization for 30 years.

In addition to his law interests, Judge Gourley had extensive mineral and oil holdings in eastern Kentucky, and real estate holdings in Florida.  He divided his time between Beattyville and Lexington and had maintained a residence here for about 25 years.

He was an honorary member of the Junior Order, United American Mechanics, and a member of the Beattyville Methodist church.

Judge Gourley is survived by his wife, Mrs. Thetis Kinkead Gourley, four daughters, Mrs. W. E. Luxon, Richmond;  Mrs. H. D. Parrish, Richmond;  Mrs. Beulah G. Spencer, Beattyville, and Miss Hattie Gourley, Lexington;  one son, Chester Gourley, Miami, Fla.;  one sister, Mrs. Eva Bowman, Beattyville;  one brother, Walter Gourley, Beattyville and eight grandchildren.

Thursday, 18 March 1943

Funeral services for Bill Gum were held at one o'clock last Saturday afternoon at the St. Helens Christian Church by Rev. Sam Wilson, pastor of the Beattyville Christian Church.  Burial followed in the St. Helens cemetery.

Mr. Gum, the son of the late James and Lula M. Gum, died at the Presbyterian Hospital in Cincinnati on Tuesday following an illness of some duration.  He was aa resident of Lee County until a few months prior to his death.

Survivors include his widow, Mrs. Callie Hert Gum, and five sons.
