BLACKWELL, MRS. M. A.
Mrs. M. A. Blackwell Died Sunday at Esau
Her Husband Was a Member of the Constitutional Convention of '74
Perryville, Ark., August 5 - One of the saddest deaths that has occurred in this county in many a day was that of Mrs. M. A. Blackwell, which occurred at Esau August 2. Aunt Mary, as she was universally known, was the wife of the late Maj. W. H. Blackwell, at one time the wealthiest real estate holder in this county. He also possessed considerable real estate in Texas and Missouri, which is now left to the six heirs. He was a member of the Constitutional Convention of 1874. The six heirs are Miss Nora Blackwell, Mrs. Prof. Burrow of Perry, Mrs. A. J. Patterson of Esau, Mrs. R. E. Nichols of St. Joe, Mo., and Mr. Henry Blackwell of Hollyville, I. T., and Dr. W. I. Blackwell of Esau, Ark. Mrs. Blackwell was one of the old landmarks of Perry county, and everyone takes her death with the deepest regret. She was buried Sunday at the Perryville Cemetery, and her funeral was attended by one of the largest crowds that ever gathered here at a funeral. All the relatives have the sympathy of the entire community. (Arkansas Democrat, Little Rock, Ark., Aug 6, 1903 - vm)

CHITTENDEN, MRS. GEORGE E.
Mrs. George E. Chittenden, who arrived here recently from her home near Adona, Ark., died early yesterday morning at the home of her parents, Mr. and Mrs. Ben Morgan, 108 South Sixteenth street. She had been in poor health for twenty years, and dropsy developed last December. She is survived by her husband and two little girls, who are eleven and fourteen yars of age. Mr. Chittenden is now at Adona, Ark., and on account of the crippled condition of telegraph service, he has not yet been reached with the news of his wife's death. Mrs. Chittenden had many friends here and at Adona, and news of her death will be received with regret. She and her husband moved to Arkansas a year ago last January. Prior to that time they lived south of Atchison and he was a market gardener. Mrs. Chittenden passed away at 4:40 o'clock yesterday morning. She was born in Sigourney, Iowa, April 6, 1876, and was married June 1, 1898. Because the death message has not yet reached her husband, funeral arrangements have not yet been announced. (The Atchison Daily Globe, Atchison, Kansas, Monday, February 23, 1914, page 6 - PT)

CRIST, JOHN M.
John M. Crist Died Wednesday at Esau.
Perryville, November 25 - John M. Crist, a well-known citizen of Esau, died last Wednesday. He was sick but a few days and died of pneumonia. He was taken sick at a timber camp near Nimrod, where he was superintending a lot of rafting for the Fourche River Lumber Co, of which he was a trusted employee. He leaves a wife and two children, a brother and many friends to mourn his death.
     He was a member of the Woodmen and Masonic lodges, which orders participated at his burial Thursday at the family graveyard near Esau. A number of Sovereigns and Masons at this and other places int eh county attended and escorted the remains to their final resting place.
     He lost a son the past summer by being thrown from a horse, and the great loss of his son, just budding into manhood, was a sad loss to his devoted father and mother.
     Mr. Crist was one of the oldest timber men engaged in rafting in Perry County, having located here over 30 years ago, coming here from Indiana. He married a daughter of the late Klingenhoeffer, who located here in the pioneer days of Perry county. (Arkansas Democrat, Little Rock, Ark, Nov 25, 1904 - vm)

HOLBROOK, DON
Santa Rosa, Ca– Don Holbrook, a long-time court reporter for Sonoma County Superior Court, died Sunday after a protracted illness related to a heart condition. He was 65.  He worked in the court's Department No. 2 from 1963 until poor health forced him to retire in 1995. Over the years, he reported for Judges Hilliard Comstock, Lester Manker, John Moskowitz and Bryan Jamar. A native of Adona, Ark., he lived in Santa Rosa for 35 years with his wife, Polly, who stepped down as concert mistress of the Santa Rosa Symphony two years ago in order to care for him.
    "I thought of Don as a wonderful teacher, and he's helped many young reporters start their careers," said Polly. "Even in golf, he could take a person's golf swing apart and give them good advice."
    In addition to his wife, he is survived by two sons, Brad Holbrook of Guy, Ark., and Mark Holbrook of Sacramento; his mother, Fannie Bell Holbrook of Perryville, Ark.; two brothers, Jon Holbrook of Dardanell, Ark., and Dennis Holbrook of Virginia Beach, Va.; five grandchildren; and numerous nieces and nephews.
