BRAMAN, M.B. and Family
Father, Mother and Child are Victims of Flu
Danville, March 6: Influenza has touched Danville but lightly this winter, but in the country districts, the epidemic has been raging for almost a month, and is only slightly better at present. At one point on Rural Route No. 1, from Danville, there are 30 cases of "flu" and pneumonia, all in a comparatively short distance of each other. A particularly sad case is reported from the Riley Creek Community, a few miles from town, in the family of M.B. Braman, an infant child having died Wednesday, its mother Thursday, and the father today; all of whom will be buried in the same grave Saturday. Two little chaps, sole survivors of the family, ,one two years of age and one four years, are now down with the same disease. [Arkansas Democrat (Little Rock, Arkansas) 06 Mar 1920, Sat Page 2]

DISMUKES, Capt. ROBERT L.
Death of a Well-Known River Man
Our telegraphic dispatches this morning announce the death of Capt. Robert L. Dismukes, at Dardanell, Ark., on Friday.  Capt. Dismukes was well known in this community.  His boyhood was spent in Jones' Bend, in this county, his father being a well-to-do farmer in the olden time.  At one time Capt. Dismukes was largely interested in steamboats here, being the owner of the Scotland, a large boat running between Nashville and New Orleans, and also at the same time owning the Monticello and another boat.  Since the war he has been engaged in the trade between Memphis and Dardanell, making the latter place his home.  He married in this city, and leaves a large family and a host of kindred and friends to mourn his loss.  [Nashville Union and American, (Nashville, Tenn) July 20, 1873]

PURSLEY, WILLIAM
The Arkansas Traveler, a New Orleans and Arkansas River pucket, ran on a sawyer on Thursday night at Marshal's cut-off in the Arkansas River, a few miles below Pine Bluff. Her entire bottom was torn off, and she went down with five feet water over her main deck. She had a cargo of sugar, molasses, salt and dry goods from New Orleans. A passenger, named William Pursley was so alarmed by the accident that he jumped into the river and was drowned. He was from Dardanelles, Arkansas. The boat careened so much that her chimneys and cabin fell overboard. She was owned by Captain John Timmons and Captain Albert Thomas, her commander. She was worth $7,000 and had on board 200 brls of sugar, 800 sacks of salt which will prove a total loss. The remainder of the freight will be saved in a damaged condition. (Cincinnati Daily Enquirer, Cincinnati, Ohio, Mar 30, 1860 - vm)

REMER, THOMAS A.
Anchorage, Ak-- Thomas A. Remer-- Former Anchorage resident Thomas Adam Remer, 34, died of natural causes Jan. 16, 1999, at his home in Danville, Ark. A funeral will be at 10 a.m. Thursday at the Egypt Freewill Baptist Church in Danville. A visitation is scheduled for 2 to 9 p.m. today at the church. Mr. Remer was born April 26, 1964, in Waldron, Ark. He lived in Alaska from 1976 until 1988. He graduated from Chugiak High School, where he participated on the wrestling team. His goal was to be a pilot, and he soloed out of Birchwood Airport for the first time on his 16th birthday. After high school, he attended and graduated from Spartan School of Aviation in Tulsa, Okla. He returned to Alaska and went to work for Mile High Geophysical on the North Slope. Later, he was employed as a heavy-equipment operator for Pearson of Alaska. Mr. Remer had been a member of the Aircraft Owners Association and the National Rifle Association. He had attended the Freewill Baptist Church in Egypt. Mr. Remer is survived by his wife, Linda Remer; his children, Marilyn, Charlie, Millie, Windy, April, Donald and Michael Remer, all of Harrison, Ark.; parents, Jack and Joan Remer of Danville; paternal grandmother, Millie Remer of Danville; sister, Ora Meredith of Harrison; and brothers, Sherman and Charles, both of Russelville, Ark. Service arrangements were by Martin Funeral Home in Waldron, Ark.  [Anchorage Daily News (AK) - January 20, 1999]

Mrs. Arline E. Rice, 28 years of age, wife of Leonard A. Rice, manager of the local Arkansas Power and Light Company, passed away at their home in the Orchard Addition, Monday morning at 5:45 o'clock following a two week's illness of pneumonia.

SMITH, (child)
A five year old son of Benjamin Smith, near Dardanelles, Ark., died yesterday from sores supposed to be caused by salivation.  A post mortem examination convinced the doctors that death results from what are known as screw worms, which penetrate the skin and had literally honeycombed the inside of the child's mouth.  The case is attracting much attention among physicians, but nearly all maintain that medicine strong enough to salivate would kill any species of worm.  [Dodge City Times (Dodge City, Kan.), November 09, 1882]

THOMPSON, CORINNE
Mrs. Thompson's Death - I am not unmindful of the kindliness of the writer, but for the interest of absent friends and relatives I want to correct a few mistakes in the obituary of my aunt, Mrs. Corinne Thompson, which appeared in a recent issue of The Chieftain.
     She was born on the 27th of November instead of March, and was the second child of her parents, my father, J. Woodward, being the eldest. Her grandchildren are Hallie and Ethel Thompson instead of Dodd. Her neice, Ella Dodd, and family live in Morrillton, Ark. She died in Russellville, Ark., at the home of her neice, Mrs. Emma Black, with whom she made her home, surrounded by loving kindred and friends and was buried beside her sister Abbe in Dardanelle.
    Those who have treasured the tress of hair, the worn out garment, will not deem it inappropriate if I add a few words yet to the memory of one I have known and loved since infancy. As “Little Grandma” she was known throughout the greater portion of Arkansas. To us, her nieces and nephews, the name “Aunt Tinnie,” bestowed in childhood, was second only to that of “Mother.” We can all, with one accord, "rise up and call her blessed." To write an obituary worthy of her were hard indeed to do. Better than all the eulogies that could be written or the perishable monuments of marble or stone, she lives in the heart of those she leaves and "her works do follow her."
    "Our dead to us are never dead Until their memories are erased; For oftentime we find our hand Doing the very thing they praised."
     Mabel W. Anderson. Pryor Creek, I. T., Oct 22, 1894. [Source: Indian Chieftain, (Vinita, Indian Territory) November 1, 1894, submitted by FOFG; Typed as it was in the newspaper]
