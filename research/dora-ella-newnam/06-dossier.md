## Answer to the research question (≤10 lines, confidence-labelled)

Dora Ella Newnam was the daughter of Elias T. “Doll” Newnam and Mary (Smallwood) Newnam (**PROBABLE**). She was born in August 1871 in Lee County, Kentucky (**year PROVEN** by her age 8 on the 5 June 1880 schedule; **day 11 POSSIBLE** from the 1926 certificate; **year 1868 DISPROVED**). On 10 December 1885, aged fourteen, she married her kinsman Samuel P. Newnam at her father’s house (**PROVEN**, Book 2 transcription). A later Peebles husband — a third marriage before Stone — is **not supported** (C087). Samuel, 11, in John and Mary Newnam’s dwelling 3 on 1 June 1880 is **PROBABLE** the 1885 groom; a same-aged Samuel, 1, in John and Elizabeth Newman’s Sturgeon house on 29 June 1870 is **PROBABLE** his natal line (Pearl’s 1910 grandmother Elizabeth). Those two households are **not** silently merged (conflict #9). By 1 June 1900 they were at Sturgeon precinct dwelling 67 with five living children of seven born (**PROVEN**, transcription). In April 1910 she is **not** in Kentucky, Arkansas, or Missouri as Newman/Newnam Dora, nor is Coy in those states, in Tennessee’s C-names, or in Tennessee’s McCoy M-window; Samuel is **not** a Kentucky Newman head; **and they are not on the original Sturgeon ED 99 schedule** (**PROVEN negatives**). Two Kentucky Dora Stones that April are the wrong women. In January 1920 she is **not** in Kentucky as Newman Dora ~48 (three other Doras rejected). She died a Stone at Ola, Yell County, Arkansas, at noon 23 August 1926, of apoplexy, informant her son Coy; buried the next day at Carters Chapel (**PROVEN**, certificate, prior session). The husband named on that certificate, T. A. Stone, is **PROBABLE** Tom A. Stone, a Missouri-born widower living in Howell Township, Perry County, in February 1920, whose marker at the same cemetery reads 1845–1925 (birth year unresolved).

## Biography (only cited claims; confidence visible where < PROVEN)

### 1. A child of eight in District 59

On 5 June 1880 the enumerator James J. Hampton walked District 59 of Lee County, Kentucky — a county only ten years old, cut from Breathitt, Estill, Owsley and Wolfe around the Three Forks of the Kentucky River. At dwelling 46 he listed a farmer, Elias Newnam, 38, and his wife Mary, 38, both Kentucky-born, with six children still at home: William, 21, at the plough; Malissa, 16; Francis, 14; Mary, 10; **Dora, 8**; and Catharine, 6. [C003][S010]

That single line — *Dora, daughter, 8, Kentucky* — is the childhood record that free transcripts never had. It matches the Soundex clerk’s continuation card (N-550, vol. 18, ED 59, sheet 6, line 11): “Newnam, Dora D 8 Kentucky.” [S001][S002] A child aged eight on 1 June 1880 was born between June 1871 and June 1872. The 1900 census would later give her birth as August 1871; her 1885 marriage return would call her fourteen. [C001][S005][S004] The death certificate’s 11 August **1868**, written by a son fifty-five years after the fact, cannot be the year: that child would have been eleven in June 1880. [C002][S003] Conflict #1.

The parents on that schedule are the couple her death certificate would name: Elias Newnam and Mary Smallwood. [C004][S003] Neighbors on the same page were also Newnams — Joseph, 38, next door, and John, 60, the household after — the interlocking clan of Sturgeon Creek. [S010] Elias, known as Doll, had been bugler of Company C, 14th Kentucky Cavalry, 1862–63; Mary Ann Smallwood, born April 1842, was a Smallwood of the Owsley line. [C040–C042] Those military and cemetery facts were gathered in prior work and were not re-imaged this session.

### 2. Thursday, 10 December 1885, at Doll Newnam’s

The Lee County marriage book, as transcribed by Debi Kendrick, records the wedding: Samuel P. Newnam, 16, farmer, born Owsley, father born Clark, mother born Owsley, first marriage, to Dora Ella Newnam, 14, born Lee, first marriage, parents both recorded as Owsley-born — a slip, Elias having been born in Clark. Married Thursday, 10 December 1885, at Doll Newnam’s, by J. E. Dunagin; witnesses Doll Newnam and Link Mays. [C010][S004] The original page (FamilySearch Q2QD-PZHL) has not been seen from this VM.

Samuel was family. On the first page of the same district, dwelling 3, 1 June 1880: **Samuel, 11, son** of John Newnam, 69, farmer (b. Kentucky, father North Carolina), and Mary, 57 (b. Kentucky, both parents Kentucky), with Eli 16, Susan 20, and Hannah 9. [C060][C061][S040] Age 11 in June 1880 is exactly the June 1869 birth of the 1900 husband and the sixteen-year-old of December 1885. The “second Samuel ~13” on sheet 2 is **Samuel McQueary**, not a Newnam. [C063][S044]

Ten years earlier, on 29 June 1870, Sturgeon Precinct household 19 held **Newman, John, 50**, farm labourer, **Elizabeth, 25**, keeping house, and **Samuel, 1**, with eight others. [C062][S042] That Elizabeth is the right age for the widow who later took Pearl. The 1870 and 1880 households are **not** the same house with a bad enumerator: John ages 50→69, wives Elizabeth 25 vs Mary 57, and the children’s names do not carry over. Conflict #9 stays open. Do not print “son of Elizabeth” or “son of Mary” as fact. The identification of Samuel 11 as Dora’s husband stays **PROBABLE**; the 1870 boy is **PROBABLE** the same child in a different house (natal vs later).

### 6. Noon, Monday, 23 August 1926

Dora Stone died at Ola of apoplexy after one day, attended by Dr. Thomas W. Beal. Coy Newnam of Ola was informant. The certificate names her spouse T. A. Stone and her parents Elias Newnam and Mary Smallwood, and sends her the next day to Carters Chapel. [C015][C016][S003] Daniels and Cossey, copying Carter Cove on 5 February 1993, found “Newman, Dora” without dates — the name of the husband of her youth, and of the son who buried her. [S078] Find A Grave returned 403 from this VM; no photograph is in the case file.

She was fifty-five if she was the eight-year-old of District 59. The certificate called her fifty-eight. The child on sheet 6 is the better witness to her beginning than the grieving son was to a date he never saw.

## Proof argument (question → evidence → correlation → conflict resolution → conclusion)

**Correlation.** The age chain 8 (1880) → 14 (1885) → 28 (1900) → 55 (1926) is internally consistent on an 1871 birth. The father’s nickname “Doll” on the 1885 return is the head of the 1880 house.
